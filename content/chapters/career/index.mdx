## Specialise after the foundation

You now have the base for any direction: front end, back end, mobile, data engineering, infrastructure and operations, security, games, embedded devices, or AI. Pick the one whose problems you enjoy and go deep, while staying able to work across the stack.

## Getting the first job

- **Portfolio**: two or three deployed projects with clean code, tests and good READMEs beat twenty tutorials.
- **CV**: one page; lead with what you built and what it achieved.
- **Interviews** commonly include a coding problem (data structures and algorithms: think aloud, start simple, test your answer), a design discussion, and questions about how you work with others (answer with a real situation, what you did, and the result).
- **Network**: meet-ups, open source, writing about what you learn. Many jobs are filled through people who have seen your work.

## Levels

A junior engineer completes well-defined tasks with guidance. A mid-level engineer delivers whole features independently. A senior engineer shapes what gets built, handles ambiguity, and raises the level of the team. The steps are about scope and judgement more than about typing speed.

## Staying good

- Read other people’s code. It is the fastest teacher.
- Learn a second language that works differently from your first.
- Go one level below the tools you use: how does the database, the browser or the network actually do it?
- Teach. Explaining exposes what you do not yet understand.
- Protect your health and attention. This is a long career.
