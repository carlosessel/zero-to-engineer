## The families

| Architecture | Idea | Used for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| MLP (fully connected) | Every input connects to every neuron | Small tabular problems, final layers |
| CNN (convolutional) | Small filters slide over the image, sharing weights | Images, audio spectrograms |
| RNN / LSTM | Reads a sequence one step at a time, carrying memory | Older sequence models; mostly replaced |
| Transformer | Every position looks at every other position at once | Text, and now images, audio and video |
| Diffusion | Learns to remove noise step by step | Image, audio and video generation |

## Transfer learning

You almost never train from scratch. You take a model someone trained on huge data and **fine-tune** it on your smaller dataset. A few hundred labelled images can beat months of training from zero.

```python
from torchvision import models
from torch import nn

model = models.resnet18(weights="DEFAULT")       # pretrained on ImageNet
for p in model.parameters():
    p.requires_grad = False                      # freeze what it knows
model.fc = nn.Linear(model.fc.in_features, 2)    # new head: your 2 classes
```

## The training checklist

- First overfit a single batch. If the model cannot memorise 32 examples, there is a bug.
- Plot training and validation loss together. Diverging curves mean overfitting.
- Tune learning rate first; use a schedule that warms up, then decays.
- Normalise inputs. Use batch or layer normalisation inside the network.
- Regularise with dropout, weight decay and data augmentation.
- Stop early and keep the checkpoint with the best validation score.
- Look at the examples the model gets wrong. This teaches more than any metric.
