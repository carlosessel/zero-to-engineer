## Attention

To predict what follows “The trophy didn’t fit in the suitcase because it was too”, the model must work out what “it” refers to. **Self-attention** lets every token look at every other token and pull in information from the relevant ones. Mechanically, each token produces a query vector and a key vector; the dot product of one token’s query with another’s key says how much to attend to it. You met the dot product in <Ref to="math" />; this is where it earns its keep.

## The transformer block

One block is attention, followed by a small MLP, with normalisation and shortcut connections around both. A model stacks dozens of these. Tokens also carry position information so the model knows word order. The final layer outputs one score per vocabulary token, and softmax turns those into probabilities.

## How they are trained

1. **Pretraining**: predict the next token over a vast amount of text. No human labels needed. Grammar, facts, reasoning patterns and coding ability all emerge from this single objective.
2. **Fine-tuning**: further training on conversations and instructions so the model behaves as a helpful assistant, including feedback-based methods that shape its behaviour.

## What follows from this

- **Context window**: the model only knows what is in its weights and in the text you send. It has no memory between calls unless you resend the history.
- **Hallucination**: the model generates plausible text. Where it lacks knowledge, plausible and true come apart. Giving it the source material is the main fix.
- **Knowledge cutoff**: weights are frozen after training. Recent or private facts must be supplied in the prompt.
- **Cost and speed** grow with the number of tokens in and out.
