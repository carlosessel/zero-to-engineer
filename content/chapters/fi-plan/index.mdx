This course has covered dozens of ideas. A financial plan puts them in order, so you know what to do with the next cedi you have spare, and the one after that. By the end of this chapter you will be able to set out the usual order of financial priorities and the reasons behind it, turn a household’s numbers into a month-by-month route through those priorities, write a one-page plan, and know when and how to review it. The household is fictional, and the order is a general guide, not advice for your circumstances.

## The order of priorities

There is no single right plan, but most good ones follow roughly this order, because each step protects the next:

1. **Know your numbers.** Income, spending, debts and net worth (<Ref to="fi-budgeting" />, <Ref to="fi-household-statements" />). You cannot plan what you have not measured.
2. **A starter emergency fund**, about a month’s spending, so a small shock does not push you into expensive borrowing (<Ref to="fi-saving" />).
3. **Free money**: any employer pension contribution you only get by contributing yourself (<Ref to="fi-retirement" />). An employer match is an instant 100% return.
4. **Pay off expensive debt**, starting with the highest rate (<Ref to="fi-debt" />). Repaying a loan at 4% a month is a guaranteed 60% a year “return”.
5. **Protect** against the shocks you could not survive: health cover, term life insurance if anyone depends on your income, and any compulsory cover (<Ref to="fi-insurance" />).
6. **A full emergency fund**, three to six months of essential spending, in a safe, easy-to-reach account.
7. **Short-term goals** (within about five years), saved in cash, fixed deposits or Treasury bills (<Ref to="fi-cash-bonds" />).
8. **Long-term goals and retirement**, invested in a diversified, low-cost mix suited to your time horizon (<Ref to="fi-diversification" />, <Ref to="fi-funds" />).
9. **Review** once a year, and whenever life changes.

Some steps overlap. A household with dependants might buy life cover before finishing step 4; someone with a very secure job might hold a smaller emergency fund. But skipping straight to step 8 while carrying expensive debt and no emergency fund is the most common and costly mistake.

## A household’s numbers

**Kwesi and Abena** have a four-year-old daughter. Their numbers:

| | GH₵ |
|---|---|
| Take-home pay, both together, a month | 9,000 |
| Spending a month, including the GH₵ 160 minimum card payment | 7,200 |
| Left over each month | 1,800 |
| Savings (instant access) | 2,000 |
| Credit card debt, at 4% a month | 4,000 |
| Kwesi’s employer will match his third-tier pension contributions up to | 300 a month |
| Life insurance | none |

Their goals: GH₵ 15,000 for school fees when their daughter starts at a fee-paying school in about three years; a home of their own one day; and a comfortable retirement.

## Their plan

They decide to do the following from the first month:

- **Join Kwesi’s employer scheme** at 300 a month, so 600 a month goes into his pension.
- **Buy term life cover** on both of them for about GH₵ 150 a month.

That leaves 1,800 − 300 − 150 = **GH₵ 1,350 a month**, which goes, in order, to:

1. **Starter emergency fund** to GH₵ 5,000.
2. **The credit card**, paid off in full.
3. **Full emergency fund** of three months’ spending: 3 × 7,200 = GH₵ 21,600.
4. **School fees fund** of GH₵ 15,000, in Treasury bills or a fixed deposit.
5. **Long-term investing** in a diversified, low-cost fund, towards a home deposit and retirement.

Once the card is paid, its GH₵ 160 minimum payment is freed, so from then on GH₵ 1,510 a month goes to the next priority.

Month by month:

- **Month 3**: the starter fund reaches 5,000, and the rest of that month’s money goes to the card.
- **Month 6**: the card is paid off. It cost GH₵ 664.73 in interest along the way.
- **Month 17**: the emergency fund reaches 21,600.
- **Month 27**: the school fees fund reaches 15,000.
- **From month 27**: about GH₵ 1,510 a month is invested for the long term. By month 40 they have invested GH₵ 21,135.27.

Meanwhile, from month 1, GH₵ 600 a month, half of it from Kwesi’s employer, has gone into his pension: GH₵ 24,000 over 40 months before any growth.

## The one-page plan

Kwesi and Abena write their plan on one page and keep it where they will see it:

| | |
|---|---|
| **Our goals** | School fees GH₵ 15,000 in 3 years; a home deposit in about 8 years; retire comfortably at 60. |
| **Every payday, automatically** | 300 to Kwesi’s pension (matched); 150 life cover; 1,350 (1,510 once the card is gone) to the next priority below. |
| **Order** | Starter fund 5,000 → clear card → emergency fund 21,600 → school fund 15,000 → invest for the long term. |
| **Where each pot lives** | Emergency fund: instant-access savings at a licensed bank. School fund: Treasury bills or fixed deposits. Long term: a low-cost, diversified fund, licensed, with a custodian. |
| **Our rules** | No new consumer debt. Any pay rise: half to the plan. Any investment offer: the ten questions, and 48 hours’ wait. Never sell long-term investments in a fall to pay for something the emergency fund should cover. |
| **Protection** | Term life cover on both; health cover; check the sum insured each year. |
| **Review** | Every January, and after any change in jobs, income, health or family. |

A plan does not need to be long. It needs to say what happens to each cedi, in what order, and what you will do when things go wrong.

## Reviewing the plan

Once a year, update the numbers: income, spending, debts, net worth, the size of each pot, and progress against each goal. Ask:

- Has anything changed: a new job, a child, a health problem, a move?
- Is the emergency fund still three to six months of current spending?
- Are insurance amounts still right?
- Does the long-term mix still suit the time left until each goal? Rebalance if it has drifted.
- Are costs still low, and is every provider still licensed?

Small, regular adjustments keep a plan alive. Most plans fail not because they were wrong but because they were forgotten.

## When to get help

A good, independent adviser can help with complicated situations: a business, a large sum, an inheritance, pensions in more than one country, or tax planning. Check that they are **licensed**, understand **how they are paid** (a fee you agree is clearer than commission on products they sell you), and that they explain things you can follow. Advice you do not understand is not advice you should act on.

## Common mistakes

- **Investing before having an emergency fund**, then selling at a bad time when a shock comes.
- **Saving at 10% while borrowing at 60%.**
- **Ignoring an employer match.**
- **Having no protection** when others depend on your income.
- **Putting short-term money in volatile investments.**
- **Writing a plan and never looking at it again.**

## Practice

**1. (warm-up)** Put these in the usual order: invest for retirement; starter emergency fund; pay off a 4%-a-month loan; take the employer match.

<Solution>

**Starter emergency fund → employer match → pay off the loan → invest for retirement.** (The match and the loan are both very high “returns”; the match usually comes first because it is lost if not taken each month.)

</Solution>

**2. (warm-up)** Kwesi and Abena spend GH₵ 7,200 a month. What are three and six months of spending?

<Solution>

3 × 7,200 = **GH₵ 21,600**; 6 × 7,200 = **GH₵ 43,200**.

</Solution>

**3. (standard)** Why does Kwesi join the employer scheme before paying off the credit card, when the card costs 60% a year?

<Solution>

Every GH₵ 300 he puts in brings GH₵ 300 from his employer at once, a **100% return** that is lost for good if he skips a month. Paying the card a little later costs only a few months of interest (GH₵ 664.73 in all), while the match is worth GH₵ 300 every month.

</Solution>

**4. (standard)** Why is the school fees fund in Treasury bills, not in a share fund?

<Solution>

It is needed in about **three years**, a short time horizon. A share fund could be down 30% just when the fees are due; Treasury bills give a known amount on a known date (<Ref to="fi-risk-return" />).

</Solution>

**5. (standard)** Abena gets a pay rise of GH₵ 600 a month after tax. Following their rule, what changes?

<Solution>

Half, **GH₵ 300 a month**, goes to the plan (adding to whichever priority is current), and half to their spending. They should also check whether the emergency fund target needs to rise if their spending does.

</Solution>

**6. (standard)** A friend offers Kwesi a “guaranteed” investment paying 10% a month. Which parts of his plan apply?

<Solution>

His rules: **the ten questions and a 48-hour wait** (<Ref to="fi-evaluating" />). 10% a month “guaranteed” fails at once (<Ref to="fi-scams" />). His long-term money goes to a licensed, diversified, low-cost fund, as the plan already says.

</Solution>

**7. (stretch)** Kwesi loses his job in month 10. Using the timeline, what can the household fall back on, and what should they change?

<Solution>

By month 10 the emergency fund holds about **GH₵ 12,435**: under two months of current spending. They should **stop** the long-term priorities, **cut spending** to essentials, keep the life cover if they can, and use the fund to bridge the gap. This is exactly why the emergency fund comes before investing.

</Solution>

**8. (stretch)** Write a one-page plan for yourself, or for a fictional household, using the table in this chapter as a template.

<Solution>

Answers will vary. A good plan states goals with amounts and dates; automatic payday transfers; the order of priorities; where each pot is kept; rules for pay rises, offers and falls; protection; and when it will be reviewed.

</Solution>

## Summary

- A usual order: **know your numbers → starter emergency fund → employer match → expensive debt → protection → full emergency fund → short-term goals → long-term investing → review**.
- Each step protects the next; skipping to investing with debt and no emergency fund is the most costly mistake.
- Kwesi and Abena: card cleared by month 6, full emergency fund by month 17, school fund by month 27, then about GH₵ 1,510 a month invested; their pension received GH₵ 600 a month throughout.
- Write the plan on **one page**: goals, automatic transfers, order, where each pot lives, rules, protection, review date.
- Review yearly and after any big change; get licensed, clearly paid help when things are complicated.
