Reading about money changes little; doing something with your own numbers changes a lot. By the end of this chapter you will have five projects that turn this course into habits and skills: a year of household accounts, a budget and debt-free plan, a savings and retirement model, a model portfolio tracked for a year, and an analysis of a real company’s annual report. Each has a specification, a checklist of what done means, and the chapters to revisit. Use your own figures where you are comfortable to, or a fictional household’s where you are not; keep personal details private, and never share account numbers or passwords with anyone, including in a project. None of this is advice for your situation.

## Project 1: a year of household accounts

**Specification.** Keep a record of every transaction in all your accounts (bank, mobile money, cash, cards) for a month in a spreadsheet, then build on it until you can produce a household income statement, balance sheet and cash flow statement for a full year.

**Done when:**

- Every account is in one sheet, with date, account, description, category and amount, and transfers recorded at both ends.
- Each month is reconciled with the bank and mobile money statements, with every difference explained.
- Totals by category come from `SUMIF`, and transfers sum to zero.
- The year-end statements show income, spending, the surplus or deficit, net worth at the start and end, and cash flow, and the three agree.
- A short note explains three things you learned and one change you made because of them.

**Revisit:** <Ref to="fi-bookkeeping" />, <Ref to="fi-household-statements" />, <Ref to="fi-spreadsheets" />.

## Project 2: a budget and a debt-free plan

**Specification.** Using project 1’s numbers, write a monthly budget and a plan to clear every debt, with an emergency fund built alongside.

**Done when:**

- The budget covers every category, with needs, wants and saving separated and a zero-based total.
- Every debt is listed with its balance, rate (converted to a yearly effective rate), and minimum payment.
- An avalanche and a snowball schedule are compared month by month, with the total interest and months for each.
- The emergency fund target is stated in months of essential spending, with a date to reach it.
- A one-page plan sets out the order of priorities, the automatic transfers and the rules.

**Revisit:** <Ref to="fi-budgeting" />, <Ref to="fi-debt" />, <Ref to="fi-saving" />, <Ref to="fi-plan" />.

## Project 3: a savings and retirement model

**Specification.** Build a spreadsheet that projects one long-term goal, such as retirement or a home deposit, year by year, with every assumption in its own cell.

**Done when:**

- The model works in today’s money, with a stated real return and inflation rate, and shows nominal figures alongside.
- It includes contributions (yours, and any employer’s), fees, and tax treatment.
- It uses `FV`, `PV` and `PMT` where they fit, and checks one result by hand or with a column of year-by-year growth.
- A sensitivity table shows the result for at least three returns and three fee levels.
- For retirement, it includes your social security or state pension estimate (in Ghana, using SSNIT’s formula), and the pot needed for the income you want.
- A note explains which assumption matters most and what you will do about it.

**Revisit:** <Ref to="fi-time-value" />, <Ref to="fi-inflation" />, <Ref to="fi-fees" />, <Ref to="fi-retirement" />.

## Project 4: a model portfolio for a year

**Specification.** Without investing real money, design a portfolio for a stated goal and time horizon, and track it monthly for a year, as if it were real.

**Done when:**

- The investment plan states the goal, time horizon, risk capacity and tolerance, target asset allocation and rebalancing rule.
- Every holding is something a person in your country could actually buy, from a licensed provider, with its total costs listed.
- Each month you record the value, the allocation, and any rule-based action, priced from published data.
- You rebalance on your rule, not on news, and record what you were tempted to do and why you did not.
- At year end you compare the return, after costs, with a simple benchmark (such as Treasury bills, or a 60:40 mix of an index fund and bills) and explain the difference.
- A note lists any behavioural mistakes you noticed in yourself.

**Revisit:** <Ref to="fi-risk-return" />, <Ref to="fi-diversification" />, <Ref to="fi-funds" />, <Ref to="fi-behaviour" />.

## Project 5: analyse a real company

**Specification.** Choose a company listed in Ghana or the UK, download its latest annual report, and write a short analysis as if for a friend thinking of buying its shares.

**Done when:**

- You summarise what the company does, how it makes money, and its main risks, in your own words.
- Its income statement, balance sheet and cash flow statement for two years are in a spreadsheet, and you have checked that the balance sheet balances and the cash flow explains the change in cash.
- You calculate margins, return on equity (with its DuPont breakdown), liquidity, debt and interest cover, and per-share and market ratios, and compare them with one competitor.
- You read the auditor’s report and the notes on borrowings, and list any warning signs.
- You value it by multiples and with a simple DCF, show a sensitivity table, and state what the current price assumes.
- You end with a verdict that is a range, not a single number, and say what would change your mind.

**Revisit:** <Ref to="fi-company-statements" />, <Ref to="fi-ratios" />, <Ref to="fi-valuation" />.

## Practice

**1. (warm-up)** For project 1, list the accounts you would need to include for a typical household in Ghana.

<Solution>

Each **bank account** (current and savings), each **mobile money wallet**, **cash**, any **credit card or loan**, any **savings group or susu** contributions, and any **pension** or investment accounts whose balances belong on the balance sheet.

</Solution>

**2. (standard)** For project 4, write a one-paragraph investment plan for a goal ten years away.

<Solution>

For example: “Goal: GH₵ 100,000 in today’s money for a house deposit in ten years. Time horizon long enough for some volatility; I have an emergency fund and no consumer debt, but a 30% fall would make me anxious. Target: 50% a low-cost share fund, 30% government bonds or a bond fund, 20% Treasury bills, rising in bills from year seven. Rebalance each January, or when any part is 5 points from target. New money goes to whichever part is below target. I will not sell because of news.”

</Solution>
