Nearly every number in personal finance is a percentage: a pay rise, an interest rate, a fee, inflation, a tax, a fall in a share price. By the end of this chapter you will be able to work out any percentage of an amount, increase and decrease amounts, measure a change as a percentage, undo a percentage to find where you started, tell a percentage from a percentage point, and see why a rate per month is not simply a twelfth of a rate per year. These are the only tools the rest of the course needs, so take your time and do the practice.

You need only a calculator: the one on your phone is fine. The next chapter, <Ref to="fi-spreadsheets" />, shows how to do the same sums in a spreadsheet.

## Percent means “out of a hundred”

A **percentage** is a number out of 100, written with the sign %. “15%” means 15 out of every 100, which is the same as the fraction 15/100 and the **decimal** 0.15. To turn a percentage into a decimal, divide by 100; to turn a decimal into a percentage, multiply by 100.

| Percentage | Fraction | Decimal |
|---|---|---|
| 50% | 50/100 = 1/2 | 0.5 |
| 25% | 25/100 = 1/4 | 0.25 |
| 10% | 10/100 = 1/10 | 0.1 |
| 7.5% | 7.5/100 | 0.075 |
| 1% | 1/100 | 0.01 |
| 100% | 100/100 = all of it | 1 |

The decimal form is the one you calculate with.

### Finding a percentage of an amount

To find a percentage of an amount, turn the percentage into a decimal and multiply.

**Worked example.** Ama earns GH₵ 4,000 a month and wants to save 15% of it.

1. 15% as a decimal is 15 ÷ 100 = 0.15.
2. 0.15 × 4,000 = 600.

So 15% of GH₵ 4,000 is **GH₵ 600**.

A shortcut worth knowing: 10% of anything is that amount divided by 10, and 1% is that amount divided by 100. So 10% of 4,000 is 400, 5% is half of that (200), and 15% is 400 + 200 = 600, the same answer.

### What percentage is one amount of another?

To find what percentage A is of B, divide A by B, then multiply by 100.

**Worked example.** Kofi’s rent is GH₵ 1,200 and his net pay is GH₵ 4,000. What percentage of his pay goes on rent?

1. 1,200 ÷ 4,000 = 0.3.
2. 0.3 × 100 = 30.

Rent takes **30%** of his pay. Always divide by the whole (his pay), not the part.

## Increasing and decreasing: the multiplier

If a price of GH₵ 250 goes up by 8%, you could find 8% (0.08 × 250 = 20) and add it on: 250 + 20 = 270. There is a faster way that becomes essential later.

After an 8% increase you have the original 100% plus 8%, which is 108%, or 1.08 as a decimal. This number is the **multiplier**: the single number you multiply by to apply a percentage change.

- Increase by 8%: multiply by 1 + 0.08 = **1.08**. 250 × 1.08 = **270**.
- Decrease by 20%: multiply by 1 − 0.20 = **0.80**. 250 × 0.80 = **200**.

The multiplier matters because changes in finance come one after another, and with multipliers you just keep multiplying. A price that rises 8% and then another 8% is 250 × 1.08 × 1.08, not 250 plus 16%.

## Measuring a change: percentage change

The **percentage change** compares a new value with an old one:

percentage change = (new − old) ÷ old × 100

**Worked example.** A loaf of bread went from GH₵ 12 to GH₵ 15.

1. The change is 15 − 12 = 3.
2. Divide by the *old* value: 3 ÷ 12 = 0.25.
3. Times 100: **25%** increase.

If the answer is negative, the value fell. A share that went from GH₵ 2,000 to GH₵ 1,650 changed by (1,650 − 2,000) ÷ 2,000 × 100 = −17.5%, a 17.5% fall.

## Going backwards: finding the original amount

Sometimes you know the amount *after* a percentage was added and want the amount before. Suppose a fictional 15% sales tax has been added and the price is now GH₵ 230. What was the price before tax?

The tempting mistake is to take 15% off: 230 × 0.85 = 195.50. That is wrong, because the 15% was 15% of the *original* price, not of 230.

Instead, undo the multiplier. The original price was multiplied by 1.15 to get 230, so divide by 1.15:

230 ÷ 1.15 = **GH₵ 200**

Check: 200 × 1.15 = 230. ✓ The same idea works for a discount: if an item costs GH₵ 420 after 30% off, the multiplier was 0.70, so the original price was 420 ÷ 0.70 = GH₵ 600.

## Percent or percentage point?

When the thing that changes is itself a percentage, such as an interest rate, there are two different ways to describe the change, and mixing them up is one of the most common errors in finance news.

A **percentage point** is the plain difference between two percentages. If a loan’s interest rate rises from 20% to 25%, it has risen by **5 percentage points**. But measured as a percentage change, it has risen by (25 − 20) ÷ 20 × 100 = **25%**, because the new rate is a quarter bigger than the old one.

Both are true. “Rates up 5 points” and “rates up 25%” describe the same event, so always check which one is meant. When you describe a change in a rate yourself, say “percentage points” for the plain difference.

## Up and then down is not back where you started

Here is a fact that surprises almost everyone. A share price of GH₵ 100 rises by 50%, then falls by 50%. Where does it end?

1. Up 50%: 100 × 1.5 = 150.
2. Down 50%: 150 × 0.5 = **75**.

It ends at 75, a quarter below where it began. The fall of 50% was taken from a bigger number (150) than the rise was (100). With multipliers the reason is plain: 1.5 × 0.5 = 0.75.

The same arithmetic means a loss needs a *bigger* percentage gain to recover. From 75, getting back to 100 needs a rise of 25 ÷ 75 × 100 = 33.33%. Lose 20%, and you need 25% to recover; lose 50%, and you need 100%. This is one reason investors care so much about avoiding large losses.

## Rates over time

A **rate** is a percentage per period of time: 2% a month, 24% a year. Two questions come up again and again.

**Is 2% a month the same as 24% a year?** Not quite. If the 2% is added each month to a growing amount, then each month you multiply by 1.02, and over twelve months you multiply by 1.02 twelve times:

1.02 × 1.02 × … (12 times) = 1.02¹² = 1.2682 (to 4 decimal places)

That is a growth of **26.82%** over the year, more than 24%, because each month’s 2% is taken of a slightly bigger amount than the month before. Growth on top of earlier growth is called **compounding**, and <Ref to="fi-interest" /> is all about it. The small raised number in 1.02¹² is a **power**: it means “multiply 1.02 by itself 12 times”. Your phone’s calculator does this with a key marked xʸ or ^.

**What does repeated growth look like?** Suppose GH₵ 1,000 grows by 10% every year.

| Year | Start (GH₵) | × 1.10 | End (GH₵) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1,000.00 | → | 1,100.00 |
| 2 | 1,100.00 | → | 1,210.00 |
| 3 | 1,210.00 | → | 1,331.00 |

After three years it is 1,000 × 1.10³ = GH₵ 1,331, a total increase of 33.1%, not 30%. The same multiplying makes debts grow, prices rise with inflation, and fees eat into savings, so you will use this pattern in almost every later chapter.

## Averages

The **mean**, the usual “average”, is the total divided by how many numbers there are. Esi’s spending over six months was GH₵ 1,850, 2,100, 1,975, 2,320, 1,790 and 2,065. The total is 12,100, so the mean is 12,100 ÷ 6 = GH₵ 2,016.67 (to the nearest pesewa). The mean is useful for planning a budget, as long as you remember that individual months will be higher and lower.

Do not average percentage changes in the way you average spending. A price that goes up 50% and then down 50% has an “average change” of 0%, but as you saw above, it lost 25%. To combine percentage changes, multiply their multipliers.

## Rounding money

Money is usually written to two **decimal places**: cedis and pesewas (100 pesewas make a cedi), or pounds and pence. **Rounding** means replacing a number with a nearby simpler one: to round to two decimal places, look at the third digit after the point; if it is 5 or more, round up, otherwise round down. So 2,016.666… becomes 2,016.67 and 33.333… becomes 33.33.

Two rules keep your answers right:

1. **Round at the end, not at each step.** Rounding in the middle of a calculation, especially before a power, can change the final answer.
2. **Rounding can leave a pesewa over.** Split GH₵ 100 into three equal payments and each is 33.33, but 3 × 33.33 = 99.99. Real systems make the last payment 33.34 so the total is exact.

## Checking with Python (optional)

You do not need to program for this course. But if you have done some Python, or want a taste, here is the growth table above as a short program. Each time round the loop it multiplies by the multiplier and prints the year and the amount, rounded to two decimal places with commas.

```python
amount = 1000
for year in range(1, 4):
    amount = amount * 1.10
    print(year, f"{amount:,.2f}")
```

## Common mistakes

- **Dividing by the wrong number.** For “what percentage of my pay is rent”, divide by the pay, not the rent. For a percentage change, divide by the *old* value.
- **Taking a percentage off to undo an increase.** GH₵ 230 including 15% tax was not 230 × 0.85 = 195.50 before tax; it was 230 ÷ 1.15 = 200.
- **Adding percentage changes.** Two rises of 10% are a rise of 21% (1.1 × 1.1 = 1.21), not 20%. Multiply the multipliers.
- **Mixing up percent and percentage points.** A rate going from 20% to 25% is up 5 percentage points, or 25%. Headlines often say one when they mean the other.
- **Thinking a fall and an equal rise cancel out.** Down 50% then up 50% leaves you at 75% of where you started.
- **Turning a monthly rate into a yearly one by multiplying by 12** when the rate compounds. 2% a month compounds to 26.82% a year, not 24%.
- **Rounding too early.** Keep full figures until the last step.

10% is one-tenth: 650 ÷ 10 = **GH₵ 65**.

<Solution>

25% is a quarter: 0.25 × 1,240 = **GH₵ 310**.

**3. (warm-up)** Write the decimal 0.07 as a percentage.

<Solution>

Multiply by 100: 0.07 × 100 = **7%**.

**4. (warm-up)** Write the fraction 3/8 as a percentage.

<Solution>

3 ÷ 8 = 0.375, and 0.375 × 100 = **37.5%**.

**5. (warm-up)** Write 45% as a decimal.

<Solution>

Divide by 100: 45 ÷ 100 = **0.45**.

</Solution>

**6. (warm-up)** What is 1% of GH₵ 37,500?

<Solution>

1% is one-hundredth: 37,500 ÷ 100 = **GH₵ 375**.

**7. (standard)** What is 17.5% of GH₵ 2,400?

<Solution>

17.5% as a decimal is 0.175, and 0.175 × 2,400 = **GH₵ 420**. Shortcut check: 10% is 240, 5% is 120, 2.5% is 60, and 240 + 120 + 60 = 420.

</Solution>

**8. (standard)** What percentage of GH₵ 4,800 is GH₵ 360?

<Solution>

360 ÷ 4,800 = 0.075, and 0.075 × 100 = **7.5%**.

**9. (standard)** Yaw pays GH₵ 1,500 rent out of net pay of GH₵ 5,000. What percentage of his pay is rent?

<Solution>

Divide by the whole: 1,500 ÷ 5,000 = 0.3, so **30%**.

</Solution>

**10. (standard)** A fund charged GH₵ 18 in fees on an investment of GH₵ 1,200 for one year. What percentage is that?

<Solution>

18 ÷ 1,200 = 0.015, so **1.5%** a year.

### Increases, decreases and percentage change

**11. (warm-up)** A GH₵ 80 item goes up by 12%. What is the new price?

<Solution>

Multiplier 1.12: 80 × 1.12 = **GH₵ 89.60**.

</Solution>

**12. (warm-up)** A GH₵ 3,200 phone is reduced by 15%. What is the sale price?

<Solution>

Multiplier 1 − 0.15 = 0.85: 3,200 × 0.85 = **GH₵ 2,720**.

</Solution>

**13. (standard)** Abena’s salary of GH₵ 4,500 a month rises by 6%. What is her new salary?

<Solution>

4,500 × 1.06 = **GH₵ 4,770** a month.

</Solution>

**14. (standard)** A litre of fuel went from GH₵ 14.20 to GH₵ 15.62. What was the percentage change?

<Solution>

Change: 15.62 − 14.20 = 1.42. Divide by the old value: 1.42 ÷ 14.20 = 0.1, so a **10% increase**.

</Solution>

**15. (standard)** Kojo’s shares were worth GH₵ 2,000 and are now worth GH₵ 1,650. What was the percentage change?

<Solution>

(1,650 − 2,000) ÷ 2,000 × 100 = −350 ÷ 2,000 × 100 = **−17.5%**, a fall of 17.5%.

</Solution>

**16. (standard)** Rent went from GH₵ 1,800 to GH₵ 2,250 a month. By what percentage did it rise?

<Solution>

(2,250 − 1,800) ÷ 1,800 × 100 = 450 ÷ 1,800 × 100 = **25%**.

</Solution>

### Going backwards

**17. (standard)** A price of GH₵ 562.50 includes a fictional 12.5% tax. What was the price before tax?

<Solution>

The multiplier was 1.125, so divide: 562.50 ÷ 1.125 = **GH₵ 500**. Check: 500 × 1.125 = 562.50. ✓

</Solution>

**18. (standard)** After a 30% discount, a fridge costs GH₵ 420. What was the original price?

<Solution>

The multiplier was 0.70, so 420 ÷ 0.70 = **GH₵ 600**. (Not 420 × 1.30 = 546, which adds 30% of the wrong amount.)

</Solution>

### Percent and percentage points

**19. (standard)** A loan rate rises from 14% to 17%. Describe the rise in percentage points and as a percentage change.

<Solution>

In percentage points: 17 − 14 = **3 percentage points**. As a percentage change: 3 ÷ 14 × 100 = **21.43%** (to two decimal places).

</Solution>

**20. (standard)** A fictional country’s inflation rate falls from 23% to 18%. Describe the fall both ways.

<Solution>

In percentage points: 18 − 23 = **−5 percentage points**, a fall of 5 points. As a percentage change: −5 ÷ 23 × 100 = **−21.74%** (to two decimal places).

</Solution>

### Up and down

**21. (standard)** GH₵ 500 rises by 20% and then falls by 20%. What is it worth now?

<Solution>

500 × 1.20 × 0.80 = 500 × 0.96 = **GH₵ 480**, not 500.

</Solution>

**22. (standard)** An investment falls by 40%. What percentage rise does it need to get back to where it started?

<Solution>

After the fall it is worth 0.6 of the original. To get back to 1 it needs to be multiplied by 1 ÷ 0.6 = 1.6667, a rise of **66.67%** (to two decimal places).

</Solution>

**23. (warm-up)** An investment halves. What percentage rise does it need to recover?

<Solution>

From 0.5 back to 1 means doubling: a rise of **100%**.

</Solution>

### Rates over time

**24. (standard)** GH₵ 2,000 grows by 5% a year for 4 years. What is it worth at the end?

<Solution>

2,000 × 1.05⁴ = 2,000 × 1.21550625 = **GH₵ 2,431.01** (to the nearest pesewa).

</Solution>

**25. (standard)** A lender charges 3% a month, added to the balance each month. What is that over a year?

<Solution>

1.03¹² = 1.4258 (to four decimal places), a growth of **42.58%** a year (to two decimal places), not 36%.

</Solution>

**26. (warm-up)** If a rate of 1.5% a month did *not* compound, what would it be for a year?

<Solution>

Without compounding the months just add: 12 × 1.5% = **18%**.

</Solution>

**27. (warm-up)** A savings account pays 12% a year, and pays one-twelfth of it each month. What rate is paid each month?

<Solution>

12% ÷ 12 = **1%** a month.

</Solution>

**28. (standard)** Kwame’s spending for six months was GH₵ 2,340, 2,180, 2,455, 2,610, 2,290 and 2,525. What was his mean monthly spending?

<Solution>

Total: 2,340 + 2,180 + 2,455 + 2,610 + 2,290 + 2,525 = 14,400. Mean: 14,400 ÷ 6 = **GH₵ 2,400**.

</Solution>

**29. (standard)** Three friends split a GH₵ 1,000 bill equally. What does each pay, to the nearest pesewa, and what goes wrong?

<Solution>

1,000 ÷ 3 = 333.333…, so **GH₵ 333.33** each, but 3 × 333.33 = 999.99, one pesewa short. One friend pays GH₵ 333.34 so the total is exact.

</Solution>

**30. (standard)** A 15% tax is charged on an item costing GH₵ 99.99. How much is the tax, to the nearest pesewa?

<Solution>

0.15 × 99.99 = 14.9985, which rounds to **GH₵ 15.00** (the third decimal digit is 8, so round up).

</Solution>

**31. (standard)** An amount grows by 10% a year for three years. What is the total percentage growth?

<Solution>

1.10³ = 1.331, so **33.1%**, not 30%.

</Solution>

**32. (stretch)** An investment rises 50% one year and falls 50% the next. A friend says “the average return was 0%, so I broke even”. What actually happened?

<Solution>

1.5 × 0.5 = 0.75, so the investment is worth **75% of what it started at, a 25% loss**. Averaging the two percentages hides this; multiplying the multipliers shows it.

</Solution>

**33. (stretch)** At 10% growth a year, after how many whole years has an amount first more than doubled?

<Solution>

Multiply by 1.1 until the result passes 2: 1.1⁷ = 1.9487 (not yet), 1.1⁸ = 2.1436. So after **8 years**.

</Solution>

### Mixed

**34. (standard)** Rice costs GH₵ 1.20 per 100 g in a small bag and GH₵ 5.40 per 500 g in a large bag. Which is cheaper per 100 g?

<Solution>

Large bag: 5.40 ÷ 5 = GH₵ 1.08 per 100 g. The **large bag is cheaper** by 12 pesewas per 100 g.

**35. (stretch)** A shop takes 20% off, then a further 10% off the reduced price. What is the total discount?

<Solution>

Multipliers: 0.80 × 0.90 = 0.72, so you pay 72% of the original: a total discount of **28%**, not 30%.

Divide the multipliers: 1.10 ÷ 1.06 = 1.0377 (to four decimal places), so she can buy about **3.77% more**, not 4%. The chapter on inflation explains why this is the right way to compare them.

<Solution>

0.025 × 18,600 = **GH₵ 465**.

</Solution>

Each month: 0.15 × 3,800 = GH₵ 570. Over a year: 12 × 570 = **GH₵ 6,840**.

## Summary

- A **percentage** is a number out of 100; divide by 100 to get the **decimal** you calculate with.
- To find what percentage A is of B, work out A ÷ B × 100, always dividing by the whole.
- The **multiplier** (1 + rate for a rise, 1 − rate for a fall) applies a change in one step; for several changes, multiply the multipliers.
- **Percentage change** = (new − old) ÷ old × 100. To undo a percentage, divide by its multiplier.
- A **percentage point** is a plain difference between two percentages; a rise from 20% to 25% is 5 points, or 25%.
- Falls and rises of the same percentage do not cancel; a loss needs a bigger gain to recover.
- A **rate** that is added again and again **compounds**: 2% a month is 26.82% a year.
- Round money to two **decimal places**, and only at the end.
