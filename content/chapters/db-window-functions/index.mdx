By the end of this chapter you will use **window functions**, the feature that most separates intermediate from advanced SQL: rank rows within groups, compute running totals and moving averages, compare each row with the one before it, and pick the top rows per group, all without losing the individual rows the way `GROUP BY` does. Questions such as "each customer's biggest order", "revenue growth week on week" and "the second-highest salary" become one short query.

## Aggregates that keep the rows

`GROUP BY` collapses rows into one per group. A **window function** computes across a set of related rows (the **window**) but returns a value **for every row**. The window is defined by **`OVER (...)`**:

```sql
SELECT name, category, price_pence,
       avg(price_pence) OVER (PARTITION BY category)::integer AS category_average,
       price_pence - avg(price_pence) OVER (PARTITION BY category)::integer AS difference
FROM products
WHERE category IN ('bread', 'pastry')
ORDER BY category, price_pence DESC;
```

```text
       name       | category | price_pence | category_average | difference 
------------------+----------+-------------+------------------+------------
 Sourdough        | bread    |         520 |              308 |        212
 Rye loaf         | bread    |         450 |              308 |        142
 Sesame bagel     | bread    |         140 |              308 |       -168
 Bagel            | bread    |         120 |              308 |       -188
 Almond croissant | pastry   |         340 |              308 |         32
 Cinnamon bun     | pastry   |         310 |              308 |          2
 Croissant        | pastry   |         275 |              308 |        -33
(7 rows)
```

- **`PARTITION BY category`** splits the rows into groups, like `GROUP BY`, but each row stays in the result.
- `OVER ()` with nothing inside makes the whole result one window: `price_pence * 100.0 / sum(price_pence) OVER ()` is each row's share of the total.

## Ranking

**`row_number()`**, **`rank()`** and **`dense_rank()`** number rows in the order given by `ORDER BY` inside `OVER`:

```sql
SELECT name, role, salary_pence / 100 AS salary,
       row_number() OVER (ORDER BY salary_pence DESC) AS row_number,
       rank() OVER (ORDER BY salary_pence DESC) AS rank,
       dense_rank() OVER (ORDER BY salary_pence DESC) AS dense_rank,
       rank() OVER (PARTITION BY role ORDER BY salary_pence DESC) AS rank_in_role
FROM staff
WHERE role IN ('baker', 'manager', 'counter')
ORDER BY salary_pence DESC;
```

```text
     name      |  role   | salary | row_number | rank | dense_rank | rank_in_role 
---------------+---------+--------+------------+------+------------+--------------
 Kojo Asante   | manager |  38000 |          1 |    1 |          1 |            1
 Efua Darko    | manager |  37000 |          2 |    2 |          2 |            2
 Ama Serwaa    | manager |  36000 |          3 |    3 |          3 |            3
 Adwoa Ofori   | baker   |  30000 |          4 |    4 |          4 |            1
 Akua Frimpong | baker   |  29500 |          5 |    5 |          5 |            2
 Yaw Boateng   | baker   |  29000 |          6 |    6 |          6 |            3
 Kwesi Appiah  | counter |  24000 |          7 |    7 |          7 |            1
 Fiifi Mensah  | counter |  23500 |          8 |    8 |          8 |            2
(8 rows)
```

They differ only on ties: `row_number` always gives 1, 2, 3, ...; `rank` gives tied rows the same number and then skips (1, 2, 2, 4); `dense_rank` does not skip (1, 2, 2, 3). Here no salaries tie, so they agree; `rank_in_role` restarts in each partition.

### Top N per group

A classic question: each customer's largest order. Number the orders within each customer, then keep number 1. Window functions cannot appear in `WHERE` (it runs before them), so use a CTE:

```sql
WITH order_totals AS (
  SELECT o.id, o.customer_id, sum(l.quantity * l.unit_price_pence) AS total_pence
  FROM orders AS o
  JOIN order_lines AS l ON l.order_id = o.id
  GROUP BY o.id, o.customer_id
),
ranked AS (
  SELECT *, row_number() OVER (PARTITION BY customer_id ORDER BY total_pence DESC, id) AS position
  FROM order_totals
)
SELECT c.name, r.id AS order_id, r.total_pence
FROM ranked AS r
JOIN customers AS c ON c.id = r.customer_id
WHERE r.position = 1
ORDER BY r.total_pence DESC;
```

```text
  name  | order_id | total_pence 
--------+----------+-------------
 Kwame  |       12 |        3900
 Kobina |       10 |        3160
 Yaa    |        5 |        2850
 Esi    |        4 |        2400
 Akosua |       13 |        1795
 Ama    |       19 |        1590
 Kofi   |        7 |        1040
 Afua   |       16 |         900
(8 rows)
```

Adding `id` to the window's `ORDER BY` breaks ties the same way every time, so the answer is stable. Use `rank()` instead if tied orders should all be returned. (PostgreSQL's `DISTINCT ON (customer_id)` is a shorter, PostgreSQL-only way to get the first row per group.)

## Running totals and frames

With `ORDER BY` inside `OVER`, an aggregate becomes **cumulative**: each row sees the rows from the start of its partition up to itself. Daily revenue from orders that went ahead, with a running total:

```sql
WITH daily AS (
  SELECT o.placed_at::date AS day, sum(l.quantity * l.unit_price_pence) AS revenue
  FROM orders AS o
  JOIN order_lines AS l ON l.order_id = o.id
  WHERE o.status <> 'cancelled'
  GROUP BY day
)
SELECT day, revenue,
       sum(revenue) OVER (ORDER BY day) AS running_total,
       round(avg(revenue) OVER (ORDER BY day ROWS BETWEEN 2 PRECEDING AND CURRENT ROW)) AS moving_avg_3
FROM daily
WHERE day < '2026-10-20'
ORDER BY day;
```

```text
    day     | revenue | running_total | moving_avg_3 
------------+---------+---------------+--------------
 2026-10-01 |    2350 |          2350 |         2350
 2026-10-03 |     720 |          3070 |         1535
 2026-10-06 |    2850 |          5920 |         1973
 2026-10-08 |    1380 |          7300 |         1650
 2026-10-11 |    1040 |          8340 |         1757
 2026-10-12 |    1780 |         10120 |         1400
 2026-10-15 |     930 |         11050 |         1250
 2026-10-18 |    3160 |         14210 |         1957
(8 rows)
```

The rows a window function sees are its **frame**. **`ROWS BETWEEN 2 PRECEDING AND CURRENT ROW`** makes a moving window of the current row and the two before it: a three-row moving average. Frames can be `ROWS` (counting rows), `RANGE` (by value, such as `RANGE BETWEEN interval '6 days' PRECEDING AND CURRENT ROW` for a true seven-day window when some days have no rows) or `GROUPS`.

<Walkthrough name="db-window-functions-running-total" />

One subtlety catches everyone: with `ORDER BY` and no frame clause, the default frame is `RANGE ... CURRENT ROW`, which includes all rows **tied** with the current one. A running total over a column with duplicate values jumps by the whole tie at once. Write `ROWS BETWEEN UNBOUNDED PRECEDING AND CURRENT ROW` when you want a strict row-by-row total.

## Comparing rows: LAG and LEAD

**`lag(value)`** returns the value from the previous row in the window, and **`lead(value)`** from the next. Days between each customer's orders:

```sql
SELECT c.name, o.id, o.placed_at::date AS placed_on,
       lag(o.placed_at::date) OVER w AS previous_order,
       o.placed_at::date - lag(o.placed_at::date) OVER w AS days_since_previous
FROM orders AS o
JOIN customers AS c ON c.id = o.customer_id
WHERE c.name IN ('Ama', 'Kofi')
WINDOW w AS (PARTITION BY o.customer_id ORDER BY o.placed_at)
ORDER BY c.name, o.placed_at;
```

```text
 name | id | placed_on  | previous_order | days_since_previous 
------+----+------------+----------------+---------------------
 Ama  |  1 | 2026-10-01 |                |
 Ama  |  3 | 2026-10-03 | 2026-10-01     |                   2
 Ama  |  9 | 2026-10-15 | 2026-10-03     |                  12
 Ama  | 14 | 2026-11-01 | 2026-10-15     |                  17
 Ama  | 19 | 2026-11-12 | 2026-11-01     |                  11
 Kofi |  2 | 2026-10-01 |                |
 Kofi |  7 | 2026-10-11 | 2026-10-01     |                  10
 Kofi | 15 | 2026-11-03 | 2026-10-11     |                  23
(8 rows)
```

The first order of each customer has no previous one, so `lag` gives NULL (`lag(x, 1, default)` supplies another value). The **`WINDOW w AS (...)`** clause names a window once so several functions can share it. `first_value`, `last_value` and `nth_value` return values from fixed positions in the frame.

## Distributions

**`ntile(n)`** splits the rows into `n` roughly equal buckets, and **`percent_rank()`** and **`cume_dist()`** give each row's relative position: useful for "top 25 percent of customers".

```sql
WITH spend AS (
  SELECT o.customer_id, sum(l.quantity * l.unit_price_pence) AS spent
  FROM orders AS o
  JOIN order_lines AS l ON l.order_id = o.id
  WHERE o.status <> 'cancelled'
  GROUP BY o.customer_id
)
SELECT c.name, s.spent,
       ntile(4) OVER (ORDER BY s.spent DESC) AS quartile,
       round(percent_rank() OVER (ORDER BY s.spent)::numeric, 2) AS percent_rank
FROM spend AS s
JOIN customers AS c ON c.id = s.customer_id
ORDER BY s.spent DESC;
```

```text
  name  | spent | quartile | percent_rank 
--------+-------+----------+--------------
 Ama    |  5930 |        1 |         1.00
 Kwame  |  5280 |        1 |         0.86
 Yaa    |  5250 |        2 |         0.71
 Kobina |  4810 |        2 |         0.57
 Akosua |  4595 |        3 |         0.43
 Kofi   |  1940 |        3 |         0.29
 Afua   |   900 |        4 |         0.14
 Esi    |   620 |        4 |         0.00
(8 rows)
```

## Where window functions run

Window functions are computed **after** `WHERE`, `GROUP BY` and `HAVING`, and before the final `ORDER BY` and `LIMIT`. So they see only the rows that survived filtering, they can use aggregates (`sum(sum(x)) OVER ()` is valid: a window over grouped results), and to filter on their results you wrap the query in a CTE or subquery.

## Common mistakes

- **Filtering on a window function in `WHERE`**; wrap it in a CTE.
- **`row_number` when ties matter** (use `rank`), or `rank` when exactly one row per group is needed.
- **No tie-breaker in the window's `ORDER BY`**, so results change between runs.
- **The default `RANGE` frame** making running totals jump on ties.
- **Correlated subqueries** for "previous row" questions that `lag` answers simply and quickly.

**2. (standard)** For each shop, find the single best-selling product by quantity among orders that were not cancelled.

<Solution>

```sql
WITH sales AS (
  SELECT o.shop_id, l.product_id, sum(l.quantity) AS quantity
  FROM orders AS o
  JOIN order_lines AS l ON l.order_id = o.id
  WHERE o.status <> 'cancelled'
  GROUP BY o.shop_id, l.product_id
),
ranked AS (
  SELECT *, rank() OVER (PARTITION BY shop_id ORDER BY quantity DESC) AS position
  FROM sales

SELECT s.city, p.name, r.quantity
FROM ranked AS r
JOIN shops AS s ON s.id = r.shop_id
JOIN products AS p ON p.id = r.product_id
WHERE r.position = 1
ORDER BY s.city, p.name;

(3 rows)
```

`rank` keeps ties, so a shop with two equal best-sellers shows both.

## Summary

- **Window functions** compute over a window of related rows with **`OVER (PARTITION BY ... ORDER BY ...)`** and keep every row.
- **`row_number`**, **`rank`** and **`dense_rank`** rank rows; filter on them in an outer query for **top N per group**.
- Aggregates with `ORDER BY` give **running totals**; **frames** (`ROWS BETWEEN ...`) give moving windows. Beware the default `RANGE` frame on ties.
- **`lag`** and **`lead`** compare with neighbouring rows; **`ntile`** and **`percent_rank`** describe distributions; **`WINDOW`** names shared windows.
