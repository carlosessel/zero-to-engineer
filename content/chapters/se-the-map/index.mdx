Coding is telling a computer what to do. Engineering adds everything that makes the result dependable: understanding what is really needed, choosing a simple design, testing, working with others on the same code, releasing safely, and fixing things when they break at night. Someone who can do all of that is valuable in any language, and that is what this course builds, from nothing.

## How to use this

Each chapter has an explanation, code to type and run yourself, exercises to tick off, and a short quiz. If the tutor box shows at the bottom of a chapter, you can ask it questions or paste your code for feedback. Type the examples by hand; do not paste them.

You will learn Python first because it is the gentlest serious language, then HTML, CSS, JavaScript and SQL, because the web and databases are where most software lives. The ideas carry over to every other language.

<Walkthrough name="se-the-map-route" />

Pace: from zero at 8 to 10 hours a week, expect very roughly a year to finish everything including the projects. The projects are the course. Reading without building does not make an engineer.

<Note>

You do not need a degree, a powerful computer, or to be “good at maths”. You need patience with being stuck, because being stuck and getting unstuck is what the work feels like at every level.

</Note>
