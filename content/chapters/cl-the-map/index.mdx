By the end of this course you will use Claude the way the most effective engineers do: as a thinking partner in the apps, as a coding agent that works through whole tasks in your projects, and as a building block inside software you write. This first chapter maps the territory, so you know what each part is for and in what order to learn it.

## Three ways to use Claude

**Claude** is a family of AI models made by Anthropic. A **model** here means a large program that has learnt, from a huge amount of text and code, to continue a conversation usefully: answering, explaining, writing and changing code, and using tools. You reach the same models in three main ways:

| | What it is | You use it for |
|---|---|---|
| **The Claude apps** | claude.ai in a browser, and the desktop and phone apps | Thinking, learning, writing, research, analysing files, making documents and small apps |
| **Claude Code** | An **agent** that works in your projects: it reads files, runs commands, edits code and checks its own work | Building and changing software, from small fixes to large features, in the terminal, your editor, the desktop app or the web |
| **The Claude API** | A way for *your* programs to send Claude a request and get a reply | Putting Claude inside products you build: a support assistant, a document reader, an agent of your own |

An **agent**, in this course, means a model that works in a loop: it decides on an action (read this file, run the tests), sees the result, and decides on the next action, until the task is done. The apps can do some of this too; Claude Code is built around it.

These are not three separate skills. The same habits make you good at all three: giving clear context, saying what "done" looks like, checking the work, and knowing when not to trust the result. That is why the course starts with how Claude works and how to prompt it, before any tool.

## What "mastery" means here

Plenty of people use Claude. Fewer get consistently excellent results from it. The difference is rarely a secret phrase; it is understanding. People who get the most out of Claude:

- **know what it is doing**, so they can predict where it will be strong and where it will slip (it has no memory between conversations unless something gives it one; it can state a wrong fact confidently);
- **give it what it cannot see**: the goal, the constraints, the files, how to check the result;
- **build checks into the work** (tests, a review, a second look) instead of hoping;
- **choose the right tool and model** for the job, and know what each costs;
- **extend it** with project instructions, skills, tools and automation, so good results become repeatable;
- **keep their own judgement and skills sharp**, rather than handing over thinking they still need to do.

Each of these is a chapter or a part of this course.

## How the course is organised

1. **How Claude works.** Tokens, the context window, why answers vary and how errors arise, and choosing a model. Short, but everything later rests on it.
2. **Using Claude well.** Prompting properly, the apps in depth, and using Claude to learn and write without losing your own understanding.
3. **Claude Code.** The largest part: from your first session to project memory, good workflows, settings and permissions, skills, hooks, plugins, MCP, many agents at once, automation, and using it safely on a team.
4. **Building on the Claude API.** Calling Claude from your own code, tools, structured output, agents, context engineering, Managed Agents, and evaluating whether it works.
5. **Mastery.** Judgement about when to trust, check or not use Claude, and projects that prove each skill.

## A note on change

AI tools change faster than almost anything else in software. Model names, prices and features in this course were checked against Anthropic's documentation in **November 2026**, and each chapter says where it got them. When something here disagrees with the current documentation, trust the documentation. The ideas (context, verification, the agent loop, judgement) change much more slowly than the buttons, which is why the course spends most of its time on them.

## Summary

- Claude is a family of models from Anthropic, used through **the apps**, **Claude Code** and **the API**.
- An **agent** works in a loop of action and observation until a task is done; Claude Code is an agent for software work.
- Mastery means understanding how Claude works, giving it the context it lacks, building in checks, choosing tools and models well, extending it, and keeping your own judgement.
- Facts about models and features date quickly; this course was checked in November 2026 and says where its facts come from.
